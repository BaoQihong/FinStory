The US economy presents a picture of monetary easing meeting persistent inflation. The effective federal funds rate declined from 4.09% in October 2025 to 3.63% by June 2026, before ticking back up to 3.88% by October 2026. Despite this easing cycle, inflation remained sticky: headline CPI rose from 2.70% in November 2025 to a peak of 4.17% in May 2026, ending at 3.72% in September 2026. The labor market is remarkably stable, with unemployment hovering between 4.1% and 4.5% and nonfarm payrolls growing steadily from 158,408k in October 2025 to 159,293k in October 2026.

### Base Case

The base case assumes inflation continues to oscillate between 3% and 4% while the labor market remains tight. In this scenario, the Federal Reserve maintains rates near the October 2026 level of 3.88% to balance growth and price stability.

*   **Treasuries:** The yield curve remains inverted or flat. With 2-year yields at 4.74% and 10-year yields at 5.13% as of October 2026, investors demand higher term premiums amid inflation uncertainty.
*   **US Equities:** Markets trade sideways to modestly higher. Corporate earnings are supported by steady employment and real consumer spending (2.50% in October 2026), but upside is capped by borrowing costs.
*   **Housing and Mortgage Borrowers:** Mortgage rates remain elevated. The 30-year fixed rate rose from 6.25% in October 2025 to 7.33% in October 2026; borrowers face sustained high financing costs.
*   **US Dollar:** The dollar remains strong as the Fed maintains relatively higher rates than global peers to combat inflation.
*   **Cash Savers:** Savers benefit from sustained positive real returns as deposit rates track the federal funds rate.

**Invalidating developments:** A sustained drop in CPI below 2.5% or a rise in unemployment above 5.0% would invalidate this scenario.

### Upside Scenario

The upside scenario occurs if inflation decisively trends toward 2% without triggering a recession, allowing for further monetary easing.

*   **Treasuries:** Yields fall across the curve. A pivot to aggressive easing would push the 2-year yield below its October 2025 level of 3.52%.
*   **US Equities:** Equities rally as lower discount rates boost valuations and lower interest expenses improve corporate margins.
*   **Housing and Mortgage Borrowers:** Mortgage rates decline. A return toward 6.0% or lower would spur refinancing and increase housing demand.
*   **US Dollar:** The dollar weakens as the interest rate differential narrows relative to other currencies.
*   **Cash Savers:** Savers see diminishing returns as deposit rates fall alongside the federal funds rate.

**Invalidating developments:** Any re-acceleration of inflation above 4% or a spike in long-term Treasury yields would invalidate this scenario.

### Downside Scenario

The downside scenario involves a "stagflationary" outcome where inflation remains elevated while the labor market deteriorates.

*   **Treasuries:** Long-term yields rise on inflation fears while short-term yields may fall if the Fed is forced to cut rates to support growth, steepening the curve.
*   **US Equities:** Equities decline as the combination of high inflation and slowing growth compresses margins and reduces consumer demand.
*   **Housing and Mortgage Borrowers:** Mortgage rates remain high or rise further, while rising unemployment increases default risks for borrowers.
*   **US Dollar:** The dollar faces volatility; it may initially strengthen as a safe haven but weaken if US growth significantly lags globally.
*   **Cash Savers:** Real returns turn negative if inflation exceeds the nominal rates paid on deposits.

**Invalidating developments:** A sharp decline in inflation or a surge in nonfarm payrolls beyond current growth trends would invalidate this scenario.

### Key Uncertainties

Three uncertainties dominate the outlook. First is the inflation-employment trade-off: whether the Fed can bring inflation down without pushing unemployment above the 4.1%–4.5% range. Second is the consumer’s resilience: real consumer spending grew from 1.47% in December 2025 to 2.78% in August 2026, but it is unclear if this sustains under prolonged high borrowing costs. Third is the Treasury market’s term premium: the rise in 10-year yields from 4.06% to 5.13% reflects growing uncertainty about long-term inflation and fiscal sustainability.

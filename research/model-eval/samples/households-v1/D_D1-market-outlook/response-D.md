**Base case: sticky inflation, resilient activity.** Conditions: CPI 12-month change stays elevated after rising from 2.70% in November 2025 to 3.72% in September 2026, while core CPI is 2.57%; unemployment is 4.1% and payrolls are 159,293 thousand; real consumer spending is growing 2.50%; the effective federal funds rate is 3.88% after 3.63% in mid-2026; the 10-year yield is 5.13% and the 30-year mortgage rate is 7.33%. Transmission: policy remains restrictive, long yields stay pressured by inflation and term premium, and mortgage rates keep housing affordability strained. Equities can hold up on earnings but face a higher discount rate; the dollar is supported by higher US yields; cash savers get high nominal yields, though 3.72% CPI erodes the real return. Invalidate if CPI and core CPI both fall for consecutive months while payrolls hold and unemployment stays low—that is the upside—or if CPI/core accelerate and the funds rate and 2-year yield move higher—that is the downside.

**Upside scenario: disinflation with resilient demand.** Conditions: CPI and core CPI decline from their September 2026 readings; average hourly earnings growth stays near 3.12%; real consumer spending remains positive; payrolls stay near 159,293 thousand; sentiment recovers from 48.0. Transmission: lower inflation pulls down long yields and mortgage rates, the Fed funds rate can ease, equity valuations are supported, and the dollar softens as rate differentials narrow. Housing and mortgage borrowers benefit most from lower mortgage rates; cash savers see lower nominal yields but better real returns if inflation falls faster. Invalidate if CPI or core CPI reaccelerates, average hourly earnings growth rises, long yields rise even as the funds rate falls, or payrolls contract and unemployment rises.

**Downside scenario: inflation shock and tighter policy.** Conditions: CPI and core CPI continue rising from 3.72% and 2.57%; the effective funds rate moves above 3.88%; the 2-year and 10-year yields move above 4.74% and 5.13%; the mortgage rate moves above 7.33%; sentiment stays near 48.0 and the saving rate near 4.1%. Transmission: higher rates squeeze housing and consumer durables, equities de-rate as discount rates rise, the dollar strengthens on higher US yields, and cash savers earn higher nominal yields but face greater inflation erosion. Invalidate if CPI and core CPI fall for several months and the 2-year yield falls while the funds rate stops rising; or if payrolls contract and unemployment rises sharply, which would make this a growth shock rather than an inflation shock.

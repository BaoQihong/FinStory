**Outlook: A Sticky-Rate Economy With Three Conditional Paths**

The table describes an economy that is not collapsing but is not comfortably disinflating either. Unemployment sits at 4.1–4.5%, payrolls rose from 158,408 thousand in October 2025 to 159,293 thousand in October 2026, and real consumer spending has stayed positive, at 2.50% in October 2026. But CPI inflation re-accelerated from 2.39% in January 2026 to 4.17% in May and was still 3.72% in September; core CPI was 2.57% in September. The Fed funds rate was cut to 3.63% by mid-2026, then raised to 3.88% by October. The 2-year yield rose to 4.74%, the 10-year to 5.13%, and the 30-year mortgage rate to 7.33%. Sentiment was 48.0 in October, and the saving rate was 4.1%. October 2025 unemployment and October 2026 CPI/core CPI are missing. These are conditional scenarios, not forecasts.

**Base case: sticky inflation, stable labour market, no fresh policy shock.** Conditions: CPI 12-month change remains in the 3.3–4.2% range seen from March to September 2026, core CPI stays around 2.45–2.82%, unemployment holds near 4.1–4.2%, payrolls continue around 159 million, real spending grows around 2.5%, and sentiment remains weak at roughly 48–55. Transmission: policy stays near 3.6–3.9%, so short Treasury yields are anchored but long yields remain elevated by inflation persistence; the curve stays steep. Equities have already risen from 6,840.2 in October 2025 to 7,580.07 in October 2026 despite higher yields, so the base case assumes no disorderly repricing. Housing remains constrained: mortgage rates near 7.33% limit affordability for new borrowers, while existing fixed-rate borrowers are less exposed. The dollar is supported by relatively high US rates, though the table has no dollar series. Cash savers earn nominal rates near policy, but inflation at 3.7% erodes real returns. Invalidate if core CPI falls below 2.45% and CPI below 3.3% while unemployment stays at or below 4.2% (upside), or if CPI moves above 4.17% and core above 2.82% while the funds rate moves above 3.88% (downside).

**Upside scenario: disinflation with an intact labour market.** Conditions: CPI returns toward the 2.4–2.7% readings of late 2025 and early 2026, core CPI settles near 2.45–2.50%, unemployment remains 4.1–4.2%, payrolls do not fall, real spending stays near 2.5%, and sentiment recovers toward the 55–56 readings seen in January, February and July 2026. Transmission: lower inflation allows easier policy; short Treasury yields fall first, and long yields fall if inflation expectations follow. Mortgage rates ease from 7.33%, improving housing turnover and affordability for new borrowers. Equities are supported by lower discount rates. The dollar softens as the US rate advantage narrows. Cash savers face lower nominal yields. Invalidate if CPI reaccelerates above 3.72% or core above 2.57%, or if unemployment rises above 4.5% while real spending falls below 1.47%.

**Downside scenario: inflation shock and renewed tightening.** Conditions: CPI stays near or above 4.17%, core CPI moves above 2.82%, the funds rate rises above 3.88%, the 2-year yield above 4.74%, the 10-year above 5.13%, and mortgage rates above 7.33%; sentiment falls below 44.8% and real spending below 1.47%. Transmission: higher policy rates and long yields pressure Treasuries, with the front end driven by the Fed and the long end by inflation risk. Equities face a higher discount rate; the index’s rise to 7,686.14 in August 2026 would be vulnerable to reversal. Housing weakens sharply as mortgage payments rise for new borrowers. The dollar is supported by higher US rates. Cash savers get higher nominal yields, but inflation above 4% erodes real returns. Invalidate if CPI falls below 3.30% and core below 2.45% while unemployment stays at or below 4.2%, or if mortgage rates fall below 6.86% and sentiment recovers above 55.

**Uncertainties that matter most.** The missing October 2025 unemployment and October 2026 CPI/core CPI readings obscure the latest turn.

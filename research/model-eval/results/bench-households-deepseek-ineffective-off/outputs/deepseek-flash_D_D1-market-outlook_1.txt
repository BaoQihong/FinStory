**Outlook: conditional scenarios, not forecasts**

The latest table describes a difficult mix. In October 2026 the effective federal funds rate averaged 3.88%, the 2-year Treasury 4.74%, the 10-year 5.13% and the 30-year fixed mortgage rate 7.33%. Unemployment was 4.1%, payrolls 159,293 thousand, consumer sentiment 48.0, the saving rate 4.1%, the S&P 500 7,580.07, average hourly earnings growth 3.12% and real consumer spending growth 2.50%. September CPI was 3.72% over 12 months and core CPI 2.57%. October CPI and core CPI are missing. These are conditional scenarios, not forecasts.

**Base case: sticky inflation, slow cooling.** Conditions: CPI 12-month change stays elevated near its September 3.72% reading while core CPI remains near 2.57%; payrolls continue modest gains; unemployment stays near 4.1%–4.2%; real spending remains positive near 2.50%; sentiment stays weak near 48.0. Transmission: inflation keeps policy restrictive, so the front end is anchored by the 3.88% funds rate while the long end stays elevated, as in the 5.13% 10-year average. Mortgage rates remain near 7.33%, constraining housing. Equities face a higher discount rate but nominal spending supports earnings. The dollar is supported by high US yields. Cash savers earn nominal yields near the funds rate, but with CPI at 3.72% the real return is thin. Invalidation: CPI falls toward the core rate while core stays stable; payrolls decline from the September 158,892 thousand level; unemployment rises materially; real spending turns negative; or long yields fall sharply while the funds rate is unchanged.

**Upside scenario: disinflation with resilient demand.** Conditions: CPI 12-month change falls from 3.72% toward the 2.57% core rate; core remains stable; real spending holds near 2.50%; payrolls keep rising; unemployment stays near 4.1%; sentiment recovers from 48.0. Transmission: lower inflation reduces pressure on the Fed, pulling long yields down from the October 5.13% average. Mortgage rates fall from 7.33%, improving housing affordability and borrower cash flow. Equities broaden as discount rates ease. The dollar softens as the US yield advantage narrows. Cash savers see lower nominal yields, but real returns improve if inflation falls faster than deposit rates. Invalidation: CPI remains above 3.5% or core CPI rises above 2.57%; real spending falls; payrolls stall or unemployment rises; or long yields rise despite disinflation.

**Downside scenario: inflation persistence plus demand stall.** Conditions: CPI stays near 3.72% or rises, core CPI moves above 2.57%, real spending falls from 2.50%, payrolls stall or decline, unemployment rises from 4.1%, sentiment stays near 48.0 and the saving rate remains low at 4.1%. Transmission: the Fed cannot ease, and the long end stays high or rises from the 5.13% 10-year average. Mortgage rates remain near or above 7.33%, weakening housing and stressing borrowers. Equities de-rate as growth slows and rates stay high. The dollar is mixed: high US rates support it, but growth fears and inflation uncertainty undermine it. Cash savers get high nominal yields, but inflation erodes real returns; if the Fed tightens further, cash yields improve. Invalidation: CPI falls and core stays stable; payrolls continue rising; unemployment falls; real spending accelerates; or long yields fall while the funds rate is unchanged.

**What would invalidate each scenario.** For the base case, the key invalidators are a sharp fall in CPI toward core, a payroll contraction, a rise in unemployment, or a fall in long yields without a Fed move. For the upside case, they are CPI staying above 3.5%, core CPI rising, real spending falling, payrolls stalling, unemployment rising, or long yields rising despite disinflation. For the downside case, they are CPI falling, core stable, payrolls rising, unemployment falling, real spending accelerating, or long yields falling.
